# Full-game review, 24 October 2026

Owner, 24 Oct 2026: "Can you run a review of the full game? ... career mode isn't particularly finished ... don't put too much effort into that. Voyage mode ... the basics are there, so you can explore, have random things happen, have dynamic weather, and you can do it potentially with friends, or at least on a desktop. And the whole connection to the shipyard, I know, is a bit clunky."

**How it was done.**
- Nine reviewers, one per area, read the code and ran targeted tests and scratch simulations (no browser or headset).
- For each area, a second agent tried to disprove every finding.
- Every finding still rated serious went to a third agent, who tried to reproduce it. All 11 reproduced.
- A completeness pass found three areas nobody had covered, and sent three more reviewers there.
- Decisions already taken with the owner (Voyage's session-only money, no duty board, the Shipyard's return buttons not built yet, and so on) were not counted as faults.

**Result.**
- 119 findings: 115 confirmed, 3 uncertain, 1 refuted.
- Of the confirmed: 11 high, 48 medium, 47 low, 9 polish. Nothing critical.
- Several findings were found twice from different directions. They're listed under each area and grouped in the priorities below.
- Nothing has been changed yet: this is the list to choose from.

## Priorities (main game chat's suggestion)

**1. Quick fixes to things that are plainly broken (small, each under an hour or so):**
- **Go to some harbours does nothing** (Voyage): the simulation hits an error and you stay where you were. It happens on Tayvallich (Ashfield), Syros (Kini, Foinikas), and 7 of the Clyde's 56 harbours. It's a one-line slip in `src/core/launch.ts` (the same one fixed elsewhere yesterday).
- **Leaving a friend's game saves their career, chart and plan over your own**, in career and Voyage alike. This one can lose a real career.
- **VR messages are cut off after about 30 characters**, and show silently on the back of the left wrist. Most of the game's messages can't be read in the headset.
- **A held throttle, wheel or joystick in VR has no breakaway.** Snap-turn or walk while gripping and the lever jumps; jump off holding it and she motors away at full throttle.
- **Desktop: W/S at the helm also move the throttle while you steer a clicked joystick** (the searchlight, the pod joystick). This came in with yesterday's W/S change. Also, holding S runs through neutral into astern: the neutral stop lasts only one frame.
- **A Shipyard design with screens placed by hand can crash the builder.** The Shipyard page dies, and in the game the page never starts. That can lock a career whose refitted boat is that design. (The builder is the Shipyard chat's; the game should also catch it and fall back.)
- **SOS does nothing on the Voyage maps** but tells you the salvage tug is coming.

**2. Voyage's basics, as the owner describes them:**
- **Dynamic weather:** with live weather (every Voyage start online) the weather never changes for the session. The forecast then announces changes that never come. Use the live weather as the start, then let the weather move on.
- **Random things happening:** rescues only come during a cargo run, so an explorer never gets one. About 1 call in 6 is placed on land or in water you can't reach. Survivors and debris drift over land in the narrow lochs. Most Shipyard designs carry none of the gear the calls need, so they silently get no calls at all.
- **Exploring:**
  - Starting the Clyde freezes for about 25 s on a black screen while its 837 cargo items are laid out.
  - A failed map download leaves a black page with no message.
  - The GPS and chart latitude and longitude are the career archipelago's made-up positions on every real map (Syros shows a position in Scotland).
  - Go's notices give career instructions ("let go the anchor" when she's already anchored, so you weigh it).
  - Go to a Clyde ferry pier moors you in the ferry's way.
- **With friends:**
  - Every snapshot is the whole world, sent separately to each crew-mate 20 times a second: about 9 ms of the host's frame, 4 Mbit/s per crew-mate, and on the Clyde about 25 Mbit/s.
  - Voice chat can go dead after being switched off and on.
  - The game doesn't notice a host who's gone quiet (tab hidden, headset off).
  - Joining from the other mode's page draws the wrong map.

**3. The Shipyard to the game (the owner's "a bit clunky"):**
- **Career boats are free:** Office > Your boats, a pasted code, or the Shipyard's "Try her in the game" put any design into your real career at no cost. So money doesn't limit what you sail.
- **No way to send a design to Voyage from the Shipyard:** every game button opens Career.
- **In Voyage:**
  - Crew > Your boat shows only your 5 newest favourites, with no paging and no way to type a code (it points to the hidden Office tab).
  - The map forgets your boat between sessions.
  - Launching the beginner boat or the gillnetter restarts you in the pilot boat.
- **Refitting the beginner boat** ignores the yard add-ons already paid for, and prices them differently.
- **Slim, deep Shipyard designs capsize at the berth** a few seconds in: the Shipyard only calls it "low stability" and lets you sail.

**4. Smoothness on the Quest:**
- **Harbour boats and buoys** sample the waves hundreds of times a frame. In a big marina, where every Voyage starts, that's about 10 ms before anything is drawn.
- **Wave sampling** recomputes the same factors over and over: over half of each physics tick.
- **The wind-sea field** is recomputed on the main thread (15-80 ms) whenever the wind shifts a little.
- **The first time a light comes on**, every shader recompiles (a freeze).
- **Thrown-away engine sounds** keep running on the audio thread.
- **Tree buffers** are re-uploaded whole, and coast meshes are never released on long Clyde voyages.
- **Many per-frame allocations** (small objects created every frame, which the browser must later clean up, causing stutters).

**5. Career:** a short polish list, at the end.

## All confirmed findings, by area

### Voyage: exploring

- **Go to some harbours does nothing: the simulation throws an error. Go to an anchorage puts you on a moored yacht's anchor spot** (high; shared code; effort small)
  - *What a player sees:* You press Go beside a harbour. A notice says you're there ('at the cargo berth, make fast at the cleats'), but nothing moves. You stay where you were, and if you were ashore you aren't put aboard either. This happens on Tayvallich (Ashfield pier) and Syros (Kini pier every time, Foinikas whenever you Go from the far side of the island). On the Clyde it happens at 7 of the 56 harbours when you Go from far away, which is the normal case: Ardtaraig, Glenstriven, Greenock Ocean Terminal, Cartsdyke, both Lorn piers and Dundarroch. Greenock Ocean Terminal and the second Lorn pier fail even from close by. Go to an anchorage (Oban's Dunbeg, Tayvallich's Fairy Isles, the Clyde's Lochhead) drops your anchor right on top of a moored yacht's anchor. You then swing inside her circle; in a 5-minute simulation the hulls came within about 3 m. After an anchorage Go, the planning table and Shipyard sign also jump back to the map's start harbour.
  - *Where:* src/core/launch.ts:73. When the port has no free berth, the fallback is `anchorSpotFor({ ...world.vessels, [v.id]: undefined as unknown as VesselState }, ...)`. That leaves an undefined entry in the list of boats, and src/world/slots.ts:157 then reads `v.position` on it (TypeError), as soon as any candidate spot has the right depth. Nothing catches the error: src/core/simulation.ts:131-137 runs straight into it, so the rest of that tick and the 'board' intent are lost. The anchorage branch on the same line uses `port.cargo.anchorage` as it is. It checks neither depth nor the harbour boats, unlike anchorSpotFor (slots.ts:158-159). The coast pipeline places an anchored harbour boat exactly at that spot. src/voyage/plan.ts:219-224: tableBerthId only looks at named berths, so an anchorage port gives null, which means the start harbour. The App notice is sent before the sim runs (src/app/App.ts:2694).
  - *Fix:* Pass a properly filtered list of other boats, as berthOrAnchor does (`Object.fromEntries(Object.entries(world.vessels).filter(([id]) => id !== v.id))`). Route the anchorage branch through anchorSpotFor too, so depth and harbour boats are checked. Wrap teleportToPortSlot in a try/catch so one bad harbour can't break a tick. Add a test that runs Go to every port on all four maps. tableBerthId could fall back to the nearest berth from allBerths(), or leave the table where it was.
  - *Reproduced:* yes.
- **Starting the Firth of Clyde freezes on a black screen for many seconds while the cargo is laid out (cost grows with ports squared times items)** (high; main game; effort small)
  - *What a player sees:* Pick 'The Firth of Clyde' on the front page. The 'Loading the coast...' note disappears, then the page sits black and frozen for a long time before the game appears. On the test machine that was about 25 seconds; on the Quest it will likely be longer. It looks as if the game has crashed. On the Clyde, every cargo delivered or lost also stalls the game for tens of milliseconds while new cargo is placed, which you feel as a hitch in VR.
  - *Where:* src/core/world.ts:185 calls stockPorts(world) from createWorld, which App's constructor calls once (src/app/App.ts:732). src/cargo/cargo.ts:268-275 fills every port to its quota (12 hand items and 3 crane items each, 837 items for 56 ports). Each spawnCargo (cargo.ts:200) runs `PORTS.map(p => waitingAt(world, p))`. waitingAt (cargo.ts:182-184) filters every cargo item and calls portAtPoint, which loops over every port. That is ports x items x ports per item placed: about 1.2 billion distance checks for the Clyde. spawnCraneCargo (cargo.ts:250) repeats the pattern. main.ts:49-50 removes the loading note in `finally`, before the App is built, so nothing is on screen during this time.
  - *Fix:* Keep a count of waiting items per port (or record each item's home port when it is placed) instead of recomputing it for every port on every spawn. Or build a port-by-item map once per stockPorts call. Keep the loading note up until the App has been built.
  - *Reproduced:* yes.
- **Go to a Clyde ferry pier moors you in the ferry's approach, and the ferry then waits offshore for as long as you stay** (medium; shared code; effort medium)
  - *What a player sees:* Go to Wemyss Bay pier, Cumbrae Slip, Rhubodach, Colintraive, McInroy's Point or Kilcreggan, and your boat is moored where the incoming ferry needs to pass. The ferry comes in, stops more than 100 m off and sits there. She never berths and never makes her 'arriving' call, so the island service stops while you're tied up. It looks broken.
  - *Where:* src/world/slots.ts freeBerthFor (around lines 98-140) picks the nearest berth that fits by length, depth and other vessels. It knows nothing about ferry linkspans. src/traffic/ferries.ts:197-215 blockedAhead stops a ferry when any vessel is within `hold.ahead` 120 m beyond her bow and `hold.side` 20 m beyond her side (CONFIG.ferries.hold, src/config.ts:2769). A boat moored at these berths sits inside that box as the ferry makes her final approach.
  - *Fix:* Keep player berths clear of each docked ferry end: skip berths inside the route's final approach box in freeBerthFor and berthOrAnchor. Or have blockedAhead ignore a vessel that is moored at a berth outside the ferry's own lane and linkspan.
- **The boat's GPS position and the paper chart's latitude/longitude are wrong on every Voyage map (Syros shows a position in Scotland)** (medium; main game; effort small)
  - *What a player sees:* On the real-coast maps, the helm's NAV page, the paper chart's lat/long grid and its border figures all give the career archipelago's made-up positions. At the centre of each map the error is about 38 km on Oban, 81 km on Tayvallich and 103 km on the Clyde. On Syros, in the Aegean, the GPS reads about 56°37'N 005°57'W, 3,460 km away in Scotland. The Clyde and Syros both read the same position at their centres. Anyone checking against a real chart notices at once.
  - *Where:* src/nav/geo.ts:12-16 toLatLon always uses CONFIG.nav.originLat/originLon (src/config/map.ts:99, 56.62 N 5.95 W, the career world's origin). The NAV page uses it through formatPosition (src/controls/instruments.ts:610, 613-621). So does the paper chart's grid and its labels (src/nav/paperChart.ts:122-125, 427-430), which also covers the planning table's chart, and the rescue, tow and fire calls' 'in position' text (rescue.ts:265, tow.ts:693, fire.ts:267). The maps' real boxes are known (public/regions/<id>/region.json bbox; metaverse.ts MAP_GEO already projects Oban correctly for the timber run).
  - *Fix:* Give geo.ts a per-map projection in Voyage mode: the map's bbox centre and offset, the same projection the coast pipeline and metaverse.ts use. Carry the bbox in the package or in maps.ts.
- **Live weather freezes the weather for the whole session, so Voyage has no changing weather whenever you're online** (medium; main game; effort small)
  - *What a player sees:* In Voyage the real weather arrives a few seconds after the start (online, which is normally the case), and then nothing changes all session. No squall builds, no fog rolls in, no front clears. That holds even if you run time at x600 through day and night. The 'dynamic weather' you see as one of Voyage's basics only happens offline.
  - *Where:* src/environment/weather.ts:159-174 setLiveWeather sets `w.fixed = true` and holds the sea (`seaHeld`). stepWeather (weather.ts:183) only moves to the next preset when `!w.fixed`. App.startLiveWeather (src/app/App.ts:5054-5063) applies it on every Voyage start, alone or hosting. The game log (docs/progress/game.md, 'Live weather ... applied') calls this 'held for the session (fixed: no director)'. That was an implementation choice; your request was to start from the real weather.
  - *Fix:* Start from the live values but leave the weather unfixed, so the director plans its next change from there using the map's odds (the Aegean table for Syros). Or fetch again every game hour or so. Keep the marine sea height only as the starting point.
- **Go's notice gives career instructions: 'let go the anchor' when the anchor is already down, which leads you to weigh it** (medium; main game; effort small)
  - *What a player sees:* In Voyage, Go always arrives made fast. The notice still says '…: in the bay, let go the anchor' or '…: at the cargo berth, make fast at the cleats'. At an anchorage, a player who follows it presses ANCHOR. The switch is already on, so the press weighs the anchor and the boat starts to drift. The notice also chooses its words by whether the port has a cargo berth, not by what actually happened, so a boat that went to anchor because no berth fitted is told she's at the berth.
  - *Where:* src/app/App.ts:2694 `this.overlay.notify(port.cargo.berth ? \`${port.name}: at the cargo berth, make fast at the cleats\` : \`${port.name}: in the bay, let go the anchor\`, 3)`. This text is the same in both modes. In Voyage, src/core/launch.ts:84-88 has already moored her (moorAtBerth) or anchored her (anchorAt sets anchor 'set' and controls.anchor = 1). src/vessel/physics/anchor.ts:4-12: switch OFF means weigh.
  - *Fix:* In Voyage, word the notice from the result after the step: 'moored alongside at X, lines fast' or 'lying to her anchor off X'. Or have the host send the outcome back.
- **Voyage: launching the Beginner boat (or picking it under Debug > Start in) restarts you in the pilot boat** (medium; main game; effort small)
  - *What a player sees:* In Voyage, Crew > Your boat > Launch > 'Beginner boat' says 'Starting again in her, where you are', and the page comes back with you in the pilot boat. 'Launch again' repeats the same thing. You can never take the beginner boat to sea in Voyage.
  - *Where:* src/app/App.ts:221-232 restartIn removes `boat` from the address when `defId === CONFIG.career.boat` ('starterBoat'). On a Voyage page the default boat isn't that one: startBoat() (App.ts:257-276) returns undefined, and App.ts:726 then picks CONFIG.voyage.boat ('pilotBoat'). launchBoat (App.ts:2705-2722) and setHomeBoat both go through restartIn. The Launch row lists every FLEET boat, starterBoat included (src/ui/mainMenu.ts:954, 791; src/boats/fleet.ts:60).
  - *Fix:* In restartIn, compare against the mode's default (`MODE === 'voyage' ? CONFIG.voyage.boat : CONFIG.career.boat`), or always set `boat` in Voyage.
- **If the map's download fails, the page goes black with no message and no way to retry** (medium; main game; effort small)
  - *What a player sees:* On a patchy connection (a Quest on Wi-Fi, a phone hotspot), choosing a map whose files don't arrive leaves a black page. The 'Loading…' line vanishes and nothing says what went wrong. This covers the Clyde's 1.5 MB package, Syros' package, any map's ground mesh, and a stale cached page after a deploy. The player can't tell a slow load from a dead one.
  - *Where:* src/main.ts:22-51. A failed fetch throws (`voyage map ${map.id}: package ${res.status}`; loadCoastTerrains throws on a non-OK response, src/world/coastRegion.ts:150). `finally { note.remove(); }` clears the note, main() rejects, and `void main()` (main.ts:58) swallows the rejection. On the Clyde and Syros, zooming in on a part of the map far from your boat shows a blocky outline drawn from the 50 m overview grid, which can be misleading. ### Voyage: random events

- **SOS does nothing on any Voyage map, but the game says the salvage tug is coming and puts you aboard the upturned boat** (medium; main game; effort small)
  - *What a player sees:* If you capsize or sink in Voyage, the red SOS button pulses and the menu says 'CAPSIZED: send an SOS'. You press it, a notice says 'SOS sent: the salvage tug is bringing her into the yard', and you are moved to the helm of the boat, which is still upside down. No radio reply comes and nothing else happens. The only way out is to use the menu's Go to a port, and nothing tells you that.
  - *Where:* src/rescue/sos.ts:45-55 sosYard() only looks at CONFIG.boatyards (src/config.ts:1555-1558, which lists only the career ports 'calder' and 'tarsk'), so on a coast map it returns null. sos.ts:65-66 then returns without a word on the radio. src/app/App.ts:1776-1783 sendSos() still calls toggleBoardTo(true) and shows the 'SOS sent' notice for any boat that isn't upright. A scratch probe on all four maps (oban, tayvallich, clyde, syros) gave sosYard = null at the start berth, and after capsizing and sending 'sos' she stayed capsized with no radio message.
  - *Fix:* When there is no boatyard, fall back to the cargo berth of the nearest harbour (the same choice gameHarbour/berthOrAnchor makes for Go), or to any free berth via teleportToPortSlot. Make the coastguard's message name that port. In App.sendSos, show the notice only if the simulation actually moved her.
- **Distress calls only come during a cargo run, so a Voyage explorer never gets a rescue** (medium; main game; effort small)
  - *What a player sees:* Rescues (man overboard, capsized dinghy, liferaft, sinking boat, divers) are the game's signature event, but in Voyage they only happen while you carry cargo for another port. Voyage has no contracts and nothing tells you this, so someone out exploring gets tow and fire calls but never a MAYDAY.
  - *Where:* stepRescue (src/rescue/rescue.ts:398-427) raises an incident only through r.run, which is set only when cargoDestination() finds cargo aboard bound for another port. Apart from the debug intents, startIncident has no other caller (grep: only rescue.ts). Coast regions carry no jobs.rescues (coastRegion.ts:115; none of the maps.ts placements set `jobs`).
  - *Fix:* In Voyage (world.voyage), roll rescues on a timer like tows and fires (CONFIG.rescue.every, out of harbour, nothing else on, honouring quietCalls) and place them with the same open-water search. Keep cargo runs as the career's trigger.
- **People in the water, crates and debris drift across land on the narrow coast maps** (medium; main game; effort small)
  - *What a player sees:* In a sea loch or a narrow sound, the survivors you're sent to find can drift onto the shore and carry on up the hillside, drawn at sea level inside the ground. The boat can't reach them, and an accepted rescue has no time limit, so it never ends. Floating debris does the same.
  - *Where:* driftVelocity (src/world/drift.ts:15-22) is a fixed east-south-east background set (CONFIG.rescue.drift [0.1, 0.06] m/s) plus wind leeway and race current, with no check for land. rescue.ts:432-460 adds it to every floating target and prop each tick, and traffic.ts:280-284 does the same for debris. Logs are the exception: they ground (salvage.ts:166). Scratch probe: of 30 incidents placed by startIncident and drifted in an Overcast wind, the first person was on land after 10 minutes in 7 on Tayvallich and 2 on Oban (some of these were already ashore when placed; see the next finding).
  - *Fix:* In driftVelocity's callers, stop a target (or slide it along the shore) once groundHeight under it is above about -0.3 m: it's washed up. Treat a person who has washed up as recoverable from the shore, or end the incident with a 'made it ashore' radio call. Leave out the fixed background set on coast maps, or make it a per-region setting.
- **Tows and distress calls are sometimes placed on land or in water you can't reach** (medium; main game; effort small)
  - *What a player sees:* On the real coast maps, about one call in six puts the drifting boat or the people on a hillside or a drying shoal, or in the next loch over a peninsula, so you can't get to them. The radio still asks you to go, and the tow then times out.
  - *Where:* startTowCall (src/tow/tow.ts:661-688) and startIncident (src/rescue/rescue.ts:236-259) try 32 or 24 straight-line spots ahead of the boat. If none passes, they fall back to the first spot (bx + dx*ahead[0]) with no depth check at all. Neither checks the spot is joined to the boat by water. placeTargets (rescue.ts:282-297) also spreads people up to searchRadius*targetSpread from the centre without a depth check. Probe, 12 boats per map out of harbour: tow on land or shoal 2/12 on oban, tayvallich and clyde; incident centre on land 2/12 on tayvallich and clyde; no water route within about 2 km for 2 tows and 3 incidents on tayvallich; 0 on syros.
  - *Fix:* When every try fails, don't raise the call; try again in 30 s, as fire.ts:300 already does. Reject spots whose straight line from the boat crosses land (sample groundHeight every 50 m), or do a coarse flood fill once per call. Check the depth at each placed target too.

- **Wave sampling recomputes its smooth position factors on every iteration and costs over half of each physics tick** (medium; shared code; effort medium)
  - *What a player sees:* The sea's height under the hull is sampled 55 to 78 times per tick for one boat, at 60 ticks a second. On the Quest this is a large, steady share of the frame budget, and it grows with every extra boat (tender, friends' boats, barges). The result is less headroom for 90 Hz and more frame drops in busy scenes. It also creates garbage, which brings occasional garbage-collection hitches.
  - *Where:* src/ocean/waves.ts sampleSurface (line 248) calls restPosition, which runs 6 fixed-point iterations. Each runs horizontalDisplacement, which calls commonFactors (shelterFactor, raceAt, windSeaAt) and, per wave (11), waveFactors, which calls arcShelter. The factors are recomputed 7 times per sample, even though they vary over hundreds of metres and the rest position moves by under a metre. src/ocean/windSea.ts:205-206 creates two closures (`at`, `lerp`) on every windSeaAt call, about 14 per sample. restPosition and sampleSurface return new arrays each call.   - *Fix:* Work out the common and per-wave factors once per sample, at (x, z) or at the first guess, and reuse them through the iterations. Replace the windSeaAt closures with inline code.   - *Where:* src/world/view/treesView.ts:632-633 build both bands with max(maxNear, desktop.maxNear) = 12,000 and max(maxMid, desktop.maxMid) = 20,000 instances, double-buffered, five models each (lines 626-629). - **The sky dome is drawn first and fully shaded under the whole sea and land** (low; main game; effort small)
  - *What a player sees:* The headset's graphics chip does wasted work on every frame, for each eye. It shades cloud noise for pixels the sea and coast then paint over (most of the lower half of the view). That leaves less headroom for the heavy ocean shader.
  - *Where:* src/environment/skyView.ts:165-174: the sky has renderOrder -1000 and depthWrite false, and depth testing is against an empty depth buffer.
