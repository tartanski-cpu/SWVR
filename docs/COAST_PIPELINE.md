# Coast pipeline: real coastline regions from open data

Status: **milestones 1 to 4 done (Oct 2026), M4 awaiting the owner's look.** Oban is in the game behind a debug toggle. The running record is PROGRESS.md "Real coastline regions".

The owner's brief, in short: generate playable regions of real coastline in the game's flat-shaded low-poly style from open geographic data, fast; the data designs the place and the work moves to cleaning and styling it. Offline processing only: the game never calls the data servers. The product is region files plus a standalone viewer to judge them.

## Decisions (agreed with the owner)

- **Where it lives:** in this repository under `tools/coast/` (the owner's choice over a separate repository), with its own `package.json` and tools, so the game's dependencies don't change. Its viewer is a page of the site, `coast.html` (code in `tools/coast/viewer/`), like the Shipyard and the hull study.
- **The core is read-only to the pipeline.** The game gets two small, general changes in milestone 4, agreed: a region may carry a height grid (physics, drawing and charts read it), and a credits entry for the open data.
- **The first region is Oban and its approaches** (owner's picture): Kerrera and its sound, Oban Bay, Dunollie, Ganavan, Dunstaffnage and Connel, about 11.7 x 11.1 km at **full scale**. In the game it **replaces the hand-built Sounds behind a debug toggle** to begin with.
- **Training mode is skipped for now** (it doesn't exist yet).
- **Data in practice:** OpenStreetMap from its main API in 0.05 degree tiles (Overpass and Geofabrik are blocked from the cloud environment); Copernicus GLO-30 and ESA WorldCover read as windows of their cloud GeoTIFFs; EMODnet from its coverage service. Norway's Kartverket depths when a Norwegian region comes.
- **Projection:** a local equirectangular grid in metres about the box's centre (x east, z south, the game's axes); a few centimetres of error over a 12 km box.

## The stages

1. **Fetch** (`src/fetch.ts`): downloads, cached under `data/raw/<id>/`; `sources.yaml`.
2. **Project and reconcile** (`src/reconcile.ts`): a 10 m grid. OpenStreetMap's coastline decides land and sea (its ways have land on the left: each segment seeds the cells beside it, each patch the coast encloses takes the side most seeds say, so a coast running off the box needs no closing). Land keeps the DEM's height, lifted a little at the shore (within 40 m) so the DEM can't smear it into the sea; cliffs stay (high ground is never lowered). The sea is always under water: a procedural shelf near the shore (the 115 m bathymetry misses the shallows) blending into the bathymetry over 300 m.
3. **Mesh and style** (`src/mesh.ts`, run after the harbours, before the package): greedy-insertion triangulation, starting from the coastline's own OpenStreetMap vertices (thinned only within 0.5 m of straight, filled in every 25 m on straight runs), a **shore band** of points 6 m inland and 6 m out (so the waterline follows the real coast crisply), the harbours' dredged basins, and the box's edges; then add each triangle's worst-fitting cell, repeating to a triangle budget. How much an error counts: double within 80 m of the shore, halving by 1 km inland, almost nothing (0.03) on land **no boat can see** (a viewshed from the sea: from a 3 m eye anywhere on the water, 32 bearings, 8 km), half in the shallows, nothing in deep water (opaque sea). Hidden ground is still covered by a few big triangles, so there are no holes from the air or from a hilltop. Oban: 43 % of the land is seen from the water. Faces wholly below 3 m depth are dropped. Colours per face from WorldCover through the region theme (`src/themes.ts`): steep faces rock, low gentle ground by the water sand, open grassland shading to heather moor with height. 2 km chunks.
4. **Navigation** (`src/nav.ts`, the brief's stage 6, run before the harbours and the mesh): OpenSeaMap's seamarks to `nav.json`. Every buoy and beacon with its category, shape, colours, topmark and the game kind it maps to (port, starboard, the four cardinals, isolated danger); every light with its character, group, period, sequence, height, range and sectors, and a chart label (`Fl(2)GWR.6s`); charted rocks, reefs and wrecks in the sea; **skerries** (islets and bare rock under 150 m across, in the sea), flagged `drawn: false` where the mesh lost them so the viewer and the game stand them up; marine farms with their outlines; and the request's hand-placed current zones, as the polygon and as the oriented rectangle along the set the game's zones use (warned if under 70 % water). Thin data is reported, not hidden: for Oban, OpenStreetMap has no charted rocks or wrecks in the box, only the skerries.
5. **Harbours and buildings** (`src/harbours.ts`, the brief's stages 7 and 8): OpenStreetMap's piers, pontoons, quays, breakwaters and slipways as the game's own kit (`BlockDef` boxes with deck heights, `SlipwayDef`, `BerthDef`): a pier line becomes a box per straight run, an area its smallest box; `floating=yes`, or an untagged pier inside a marina, is a pontoon; a quay (usually a stretch of coastline tagged `man_made=quay`) a wall of boxes reaching 10 m inland from the real face. The water within 30 m of each piece is **dredged** to working depth (the procedural shelf would make harbours beaches), and at a quay the ground drops straight down at the face. Berths along every face that looks onto open water, one every 16 m. Pieces within 250 m make one harbour, named from its marina, terminal or named pier, else the nearest place. **Every harbour in the data is modelled** (the owner's rule: a real-coast map is never cut down to the hand-built regions' three harbours); each one with a berth becomes a port the game sends work to (the Clyde: 78 harbours, 59 ports; the rest are drying piers and slips with no water alongside, drawn but not worked). The **spine roles** only label which harbour plays which part; they never limit the list. **Spine roles** by name or `auto` (sheltered harbour: least fetch; exposed pier: most; anchorage: the most sheltered 4-15 m water clear of harbours and marks). **Buildings** within 400 m of a harbour: each footprint as its smallest box with a pitched roof (sheds flatter), height from tags or by kind, theme colours. **Hand edits** in `requests/<id>.edits.yaml` (remove, change, add), applied every run.
6. **Package** (`src/package.ts`, the brief's stage 10, run last): the region in the game's own shapes (`RegionDef`: ports with cargo and fuel berths, stacks, lamps and floods, trade from the request's `economy`; the kit as `BlockDef`s, pontoons as low piers; buildings; slipways; marks with a game kind; lights, one colour each since the game has no sector lights yet; race zones; shelter by fetch; fishing grounds for the request's fish; potting grounds round the skerries; landmarks: the highest named summit and the tallest lighthouse) to `src/world/regions/<id>.coast.json`, in the region's own metres. The game places it (`regions/oban.ts`) and fetches the terrain.
   The mesh stage also, for the game: fades land and sea at the box's edges to the game's open-sea floor (40 m) over 400 m, so the region ends in open sea as its islands do; keeps the deep faces (not drawn) after the chunks for the physics; stands the lost skerries back up in the mesh; and **fails** (exit code 1, and a warning in the report) if a chunk is over 12,000 triangles. The game's tests check the same, so the site's build fails too.

## Houses

The buildings step (`harbours.ts`, with `houses.ts`) gives each footprint near a harbour a type (cottage, house, terrace, shed, church, warehouse, boathouse, castle, ruin), floors, a roof shape and colours: from its OpenStreetMap tags where present, else by rules (footprint size and shape, shared walls, landuse, quays and shore). Theme defaults are in `themes.ts` `houses`. The game builds each type with its own generator (`src/world/view/houseKit.ts`), windows painted on (the `facade` tile, lit at night). The report's `houses` gives counts by type and the share from tags.

## Trees

The trees stage (`trees.ts`, after the mesh) writes `forest.bin`: a 20 m grid, a byte a cell (kind of wood in the high four bits, density in the low four), run-length packed, from OpenStreetMap's woods (`landuse=forest`, `natural=wood`, `natural=scrub`, `landuse=orchard`, by `leaf_type`) over the satellite land cover's trees and shrubs, cleared above the treeline, on steep ground, at the shore, on roads and round buildings. `trees.json` (in the package as `trees`) holds each kind's spacing, rows and species mix and the species' colours (`themes.ts` `forest`). The game scatters the trees near the viewer from it (`src/world/view/treesView.ts`).

The boats stage (`boats.ts`, after harbours) writes `boats.json` (in the package as `boats`): where harbour boats lie on moorings and at anchor. It takes OpenStreetMap's mooring buoys (`seamark:type=mooring`) where mapped. Few are, so it also lays a mooring field in each harbour's sheltered water, of the right depth, clear of the shore, the piers, the berths, the marinas and each harbour's fairway to sea. It puts a few boats in each anchorage, and adds the theme's boat mix and hull colours (`themes.ts` `boats`). The game adds boats alongside a share of the berths itself, and draws and collides with them all (`src/world/harbourBoats.ts`, `src/world/view/harbourBoatsView.ts`).

The features stage (`features.ts`, after nav) writes `features.json`: everything else in the map data the game's later additions use. That's marine farms, landmarks (castles, towers, masts, chimneys, monuments, lighthouses, wind turbines, churches), the shore (beaches by surface, shingle, bare rock, cliff lines), farmland, ranked place and sea names, fog signals, wrecks, submarine cables and pipelines, power-line spans over the water with any charted clearance, and ferry routes and terminals. The package names the file and its counts (`features`); the game fetches it when needed. Nothing is guessed, and thin data is listed in the report. The package stage also warns where piers or quays overlap at different deck heights over 40 m² or more.

## Names

The game's names and ids are in the Latin alphabet. A feature's name is its `name:en`, else its `int_name`, else its own name transliterated (Greek, ELOT 743 simplified: `common.ts` `nameOf`, `latin`; ids from `slug`). Rename anything clumsy in the region's edits file (Syros: the harbours are named after their fishing shelters in the map data).

## Big maps, in chunks (Voyage mode only)

A request with `chunked: true` (the Clyde, 36 x 37 km) is written for streaming:

- **Mesh:**
  - The triangle budget goes by area (`budgetPerKm2`).
  - It writes a file per 2 km chunk, `chunks/<n>.bin`: Uint32 vertex and face counts, Float32 vertices (region metres), Uint32 faces (drawn first, then deep), RGB bytes per face.
  - It also writes `coarse.bin`: Int16 heights in decimetres then RGB bytes, every `coarseCell` m, rasterised from the same mesh.
- **Package:** goes to `public/regions/<id>/package.json`. The game fetches it when the map is chosen (`main.ts`), so Career mode never carries it. The map's line in `world/maps.ts` has `remote` (where it goes) instead of `region`.
- **In the game:** the coarse grid is the ground everywhere until a chunk is streamed in round the eye or a boat (`world/coastStream.ts`, `regions.coastLoadDistance` / `coastUnloadDistance`).
- **Drawing:** the harbour kit is drawn in 1 km squares near the viewer only (`coastStructureCell`, `coastStructureDistance`).
- **The map viewer** (`coast.html`) doesn't read chunked maps yet.

## Milestones (the owner's)

1. **Pipeline skeleton and the first box** (done): fetch, project, reconcile, mesh, colour; the viewer.
2. **Navigation and hazards** (done): seamarks to buoys, lights with characteristics, charted rocks and wrecks (and skerries), the hand-placed race zone (the Falls of Lora at Connel). In the viewer: the marks in their shapes and colours, the lights flashing their characters (sector lights by bearing), labels, night, and a test boat to drive by the marks.
3. **Harbours and buildings** (done): harbour kit from OSM outlines, spine roles, building blocks near harbours, the hand-edit overlay. Test: tie up at a real pier (the town quay at Oban, headless). Also, on the owner's word: more triangles at the coast, fewer on hidden ground.
4. **Package and load in the game** (done): the region in the game behind a debug toggle (in place of the Sounds, and the Fjord, which its full-scale box covers), its mesh the ground for the physics, the drawing and the charts, distant chunks coarser, buildings near only, the budget failing the build, the credits in the menu. Test: sail from home into Oban (headless: the way in from Calder Haven is open water, within the triangle budget all the way; the Quest's frame rate is the owner's check).
5. **Second and third regions:** a Norwegian fjord (Kartverket depths); the German training harbour is skipped for now. Done first, at the owner's ask: **Tayvallich and Loch Sween** (`requests/tayvallich.yaml`), in the game by the same test toggle (`?coast=tayvallich`). Fetch now joins height tiles where a box crosses a 1-degree line (this one crosses 56 N); a request can place its anchorage by name and position (the Fairy Isles, which the automatic choice wouldn't pick).
