# Coast pipeline: real coastline regions from open data

Status: **milestones 1 to 3 done (Oct 2026), M3 awaiting the owner's look.** The running record is PROGRESS.md "Real coastline regions".

The owner's brief, in short: generate playable regions of real coastline in the game's flat-shaded low-poly style from open geographic data, fast; the data designs the place and the work moves to cleaning and styling it. Offline processing only: the game never calls the data servers. The product is region files plus a standalone viewer to judge them.

## Decisions (agreed with the owner)

- **Where it lives:** in this repository under `tools/coast/` (the owner's choice over a separate repository), with its own `package.json` and tools, so the game's dependencies don't change. Its viewer is a page of the site, `coast.html` (code in `tools/coast/viewer/`), like the Shipyard and the hull study.
- **The core is read-only to the pipeline.** The game gets two small, general changes in milestone 4, agreed: a region may carry a height grid (physics, drawing and charts read it), and a credits entry for the open data.
- **The first region is Oban and its approaches** (owner's picture): Kerrera and its sound, Oban Bay, Dunollie, Ganavan, Dunstaffnage and Connel, about 11.7 x 11.1 km at **full scale**. In the game it **replaces the hand-built Sounds behind a debug toggle** to begin with.
- **Training mode is skipped for now** (it doesn't exist yet).
- **Data in practice:** OpenStreetMap from its main API in 0.05 degree tiles (Overpass and Geofabrik are blocked from the cloud environment); Copernicus GLO-30 and ESA WorldCover read as windows of their cloud GeoTIFFs; EMODnet from its coverage service. Norway's Kartverket depths when a Norwegian region comes.
- **Projection:** a local equirectangular grid in metres about the box's centre (x east, z south, the game's axes); a few centimetres of error over a 12 km box.

## The stages

1. **Fetch** (`src/fetch.ts`): downloads, cached under `data/raw/<id>/`; `sources.yaml`.
2. **Project and reconcile** (`src/reconcile.ts`): a 10 m grid. OpenStreetMap's coastline decides land and sea (its ways have land on the left: each segment seeds the cells beside it, each patch the coast encloses takes the side most seeds say, so a coast running off the box needs no closing). Land keeps the DEM's height, lifted a little at the shore (within 40 m) so the DEM can't smear it into the sea; cliffs stay (high ground is never lowered). The sea is always under water: a procedural shelf near the shore (the 115 m bathymetry misses the shallows) blending into the bathymetry over 300 m.
3. **Mesh and style** (`src/mesh.ts`, run last): greedy-insertion triangulation, starting from the coastline's own OpenStreetMap vertices (thinned only within 0.5 m of straight, filled in every 25 m on straight runs), a **shore band** of points 6 m inland and 6 m out (so the waterline follows the real coast crisply), the harbours' dredged basins, and the box's edges; then add each triangle's worst-fitting cell, repeating to a triangle budget. How much an error counts: double within 80 m of the shore, halving by 1 km inland, almost nothing (0.03) on land **no boat can see** (a viewshed from the sea: from a 3 m eye anywhere on the water, 32 bearings, 8 km), half in the shallows, nothing in deep water (opaque sea). Hidden ground is still covered by a few big triangles, so there are no holes from the air or from a hilltop. Oban: 43 % of the land is seen from the water. Faces wholly below 3 m depth are dropped. Colours per face from WorldCover through the region theme (`src/themes.ts`): steep faces rock, low gentle ground by the water sand, open grassland shading to heather moor with height. 2 km chunks.
4. **Navigation** (`src/nav.ts`, the brief's stage 6, run before the harbours and the mesh): OpenSeaMap's seamarks to `nav.json`. Every buoy and beacon with its category, shape, colours, topmark and the game kind it maps to (port, starboard, the four cardinals, isolated danger); every light with its character, group, period, sequence, height, range and sectors, and a chart label (`Fl(2)GWR.6s`); charted rocks, reefs and wrecks in the sea; **skerries** (islets and bare rock under 150 m across, in the sea), flagged `drawn: false` where the mesh lost them so the viewer and the game stand them up; marine farms with their outlines; and the request's hand-placed current zones, as the polygon and as the oriented rectangle along the set the game's zones use (warned if under 70 % water). Thin data is reported, not hidden: for Oban, OpenStreetMap has no charted rocks or wrecks in the box, only the skerries.
5. **Harbours and buildings** (`src/harbours.ts`, the brief's stages 7 and 8): OpenStreetMap's piers, pontoons, quays, breakwaters and slipways as the game's own kit (`BlockDef` boxes with deck heights, `SlipwayDef`, `BerthDef`): a pier line becomes a box per straight run, an area its smallest box; `floating=yes`, or an untagged pier inside a marina, is a pontoon; a quay (usually a stretch of coastline tagged `man_made=quay`) a wall of boxes reaching 10 m inland from the real face. The water within 30 m of each piece is **dredged** to working depth (the procedural shelf would make harbours beaches), and at a quay the ground drops straight down at the face. Berths along every face that looks onto open water, one every 16 m. Pieces within 250 m make one harbour, named from its marina, terminal or named pier, else the nearest place. **Spine roles** by name or `auto` (sheltered harbour: least fetch; exposed pier: most; anchorage: the most sheltered 4-15 m water clear of harbours and marks). **Buildings** within 400 m of a harbour: each footprint as its smallest box with a pitched roof (sheds flatter), height from tags or by kind, theme colours. **Hand edits** in `requests/<id>.edits.yaml` (remove, change, add), applied every run.

## Milestones (the owner's)

1. **Pipeline skeleton and the first box** (done): fetch, project, reconcile, mesh, colour; the viewer.
2. **Navigation and hazards** (done): seamarks to buoys, lights with characteristics, charted rocks and wrecks (and skerries), the hand-placed race zone (the Falls of Lora at Connel). In the viewer: the marks in their shapes and colours, the lights flashing their characters (sector lights by bearing), labels, night, and a test boat to drive by the marks.
3. **Harbours and buildings** (done): harbour kit from OSM outlines, spine roles, building blocks near harbours, the hand-edit overlay. Test: tie up at a real pier (the town quay at Oban, headless). Also, on the owner's word: more triangles at the coast, fewer on hidden ground.
4. **Package and load in the game:** the height-grid region and credits in the game, distant chunks coarser, the budget report failing the build, sail in from home (the Sounds' toggle).
5. **Second and third regions:** a Norwegian fjord (Kartverket depths); the German training harbour is skipped for now.
