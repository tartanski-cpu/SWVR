# Training mode: a plan

The owner's brief (Oct 2026): a third way to play, **Training**, "small harbour maps with session recording and review", a placeholder tile on the front page until now. The owner asked (30 Oct 2026) for a plan built from the **RYA Powerboat Scheme** and the **German Sportbootführerschein (SBF)**. This is that plan: what the two schemes ask of a trainee, what the game can already judge, the course the game would teach, the harbour it needs, how a session is recorded and reviewed, and the order to build it in. **Milestones 1 to 4 are built** (30 and 31 Oct 2026: the training harbour; Harbour and Rescue practice complete with the Examiner's Run and badges; every attempt recorded and reviewed on the chart; springs, mooring buoys, a chosen anchor scope and the kill cord added to the game; Coast practice, C1 to C7, with a bearing readout in the binoculars; `docs/progress/training.md`); the instructor (M5), Passage practice (M6) and the rendered replay (M7) are the plan.

How it was made: the two syllabi were researched from their primary sources (the RYA's own course pages and centre transcriptions of the G20 logbook; the SpFV with its Anlagen 3, 4 and 5, the ELWIS scoring key and question catalogues, the DMYV/DSV examiner guideline) and checked a second time; the game was audited topic by topic against them; two plans were drafted from opposite ends (course first, engine first) and a critic merged them. Sources are listed at the end.

**One rule above all:** the game is practice modelled on the two schemes. It is not a course, and nothing it awards is a certificate. The courses and badges have the game's own names; each exercise's brief says which syllabus item it practises; the Training page carries one line saying so.

## 1. What the two schemes ask

The two schemes package things differently but agree on the practical core.

**RYA Powerboat Scheme** (UK) is a ladder of course-completion certificates, assessed by the instructor as you go: **Level 1** (one day), **Level 2** (two days; the National Powerboat Certificate, the basis of the ICC, endorsed with the type of boat and "coastal" if taught on tidal water), **Intermediate** (two days, planing boat, coastal passages by day), **Advanced** (two days with a night passage), and the only examined one, the **RYA/MCA Advanced Powerboat Certificate of Competence** (a practical exam by day and night, 4 to 7 hours). Side branches: Safety Boat and Tender Operator.

- **Level 2 "can" items** (the core): pre-start checks, start and stop; use the kill cord at all times; low-speed manoeuvres including turning in a confined area, the effect of wind on the bow, and holding off; high-speed S- and U-turns; approach and secure to a mooring buoy; approach and anchor, check holding, weigh; make fast alongside, use springs, leave ahead or astern; man overboard by both methods (into-wind and drift-down), with awareness of the propeller and the engine off at the pick-up.
- **Level 2 knowledge** (not practical): launching and trailers, types of craft and engines, IRPCS rules 5 to 9 and 12 to 19, sound signals, distress and the Mayday, fire, towing and being towed, rope work; and Section C (coastal): charts and buoyage, tides and streams, steering and hand-bearing compasses, pilotage and passage planning.
- **Intermediate** adds pilotage in local waters, a practical passage, fixing position by eye and by GPS, Med-style mooring (knowledge), and the chartwork of Day Skipper (course to steer, tidal heights, waypoints with XTE, SOG, COG).
- **Advanced** adds rough weather and trim, towing in confined waters, holding station, all berthing, pilotage plans with leading and clearing lines, high-speed navigation, night cruising, search patterns, emergencies, and engine fault finding. Its assessment form is "enter and leave a charted port by day and by night and explain your plan".
- **Standard of judgement** (Level 2 direct assessment wording): "failure to complete a manoeuvre successfully at the first attempt will not necessarily result in overall failure, but a timely awareness of the need to abort an exercise and try again is important."

**Sportbootführerschein** (Germany) is one licence per water (Binnen, See), each with a written paper and a practical exam, run by the DMYV and DSV to the SpFV.

- **Theory:** 30 four-choice questions (7 basic, 23 specific), marked separately; See adds a chart task of nine parts on practice chart D49 (course to steer with variation and deviation, a cross-bearing fix, dead reckoning, set and drift, the meaning of a mark and its light).
- **Practical (Anlage 4, the Praxisprotokoll):** **compulsory** manoeuvres, all must pass: man overboard under engine, coming alongside, leaving the berth, and for See also steering a compass course and taking a bearing (single or cross). **Other** manoeuvres, up to three set, two must pass: stopping on course (the propeller's indirect steering effect astern), turning in a confined space, steering by marks or landmarks, putting on a lifejacket, and the right manoeuvring sound signal (one, two or three short blasts). **Knots:** six of up to seven asked from nine named knots, with their use explained. Every item has two attempts and is marked only "sufficient / not sufficient", with a written reason for any fail.
- **The examiner's criteria** (DMYV/DSV guideline, Anlage 7): at the MOB call the drive is disengaged at once, the stern is turned away, the command "rescue gear ready and a lookout" is given, the pick-up side is named, and the boat stops beside the casualty with the propeller stopped. Coming alongside is done with rudder and engine only, stopped at the berth out of gear; pulling her in by hand or boat hook, or drifting onto the berth, is not allowed. Steering a course means "steady on course over a set distance" with course changes carried out at once.
- Above it the **SKS** (coastal, 12 miles) adds anchoring as a manoeuvre, lines when berthing (springs, slip lines), an engine check and a weather reading.

What they share, and what the game's course is built on: engine and lines; low-speed control (stop, turn, hold off); berthing and leaving; man overboard; anchoring; steering a course and by marks; lights and sound signals; chartwork and pilotage; then passages by day and night. What they ask that no simulation should pretend to do: knots, the lifejacket, launching and trailers, the written papers.

## 2. What the game can already judge

The audit (every topic checked in the code) in short. **Modelled and usable as it is:** hull handling with an emergent pivot point and measured turning circles and crash stops (`vessel/physics`, `seaTrials.ts`); prop walk on the old workboat (`propWalkAstern` 0.12); rudder in the prop wash, and almost nothing astern; twin screws, pod drives with the joystick, azimuth drives on the tug; windage with the centre of effort shifting so a stopped boat lies beam-on; the trolling valve; berthing with bow and stern lines, the docking hot spot, fixed and roving fenders, quay fenders and contact speeds (`VesselState.contact`); anchoring with holding by bottom type, dragging and the windlass; the life ring and survivor recovery with the propeller rule and the prop-strike model; towing astern with snatch loads and parting; fog signals as a duty (a prolonged blast every two minutes under way); IALA A lateral, cardinal, isolated-danger and special marks with their light rhythms; the paper chart and plotter, the planning chart with routes; currents in race zones with set and drift; weather by hand and by preset, time of day; the cold start as a checklist duty; night panel lighting; fire fighting; engine overheating and warning lamps; a walkable quay with screens and posters; and the first-voyage tutorial (`career/tutorial.ts`), which is already the shape an exercise needs: a step is a plain check on the world, announced once over the VHF and ticked on the duty board.

**Missing, so either left out or asked for:** picking up a mooring buoy; springs and slip lines; alongside (hip) towing; the kill cord; trim tabs and power trim; a hand-bearing compass (no way to take a bearing by eye); magnetic variation (headings are true); tide height (no rise and fall); safe-water marks and sector lights; manoeuvring sound signals (the horn exists, nothing counts short blasts); COLREG give-way behaviour by other boats; knots; the player's own lifejacket; any recording, replay or scoring of a session.

**One finding that decides the training boat** (corrected while building M1). "Stopping on course" and "turning in a confined space" are lessons in prop walk, so they need a shaft-and-rudder boat that has it. The audit said every Shipyard-built boat had prop walk set to zero; it was wrong. The zero in `config/modules.ts` is the outboard module's own, used only by the RIB. Every Shipyard boat is built from the landing craft's recipe (`boats/landingCraft.json`), whose shaft propeller walks: `propWalkAhead` 0.03, `propWalkAstern` 0.12, right-handed, the old workboat's figures. So the fishing boat already walks her stern to port going astern, and no change was needed. Measured in M1: a crash stop from 4.8 kn with the wheel amidships swings her 18° off her heading; stopped with the walk corrected, 7°. Twin-screw boats have none (counter-rotating). The career's beginner boat is a stern drive (the outboard module, no walk), right for a stern drive.

## 3. The course

Four practice courses of short exercises, each a few minutes afloat, picked from a **Training** tab in the menu or the front-page tile. Each exercise starts fresh: the boat placed at a berth or a spot, the wind and time of day set, the player aboard. Each ends **"passed" or "try again"** with the reason and the handful of numbers that matter (an examiner's verdict, not a leaderboard); two attempts, as both schemes give; aborting a bad approach and going round again is never a fault. A weighted fault total sits underneath for the badge (bronze, silver, gold). A course badge when every exercise in it has passed once.

### Course 1: Harbour practice (RYA Level 1 and 2 handling; the SBF compulsory and other manoeuvres)

| # | Exercise | Practises | The player | Passed when | Needs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | **Start her up** | RYA L1/L2 pre-start checks, start and stop; SBF basic questions 52 to 57 | Cold boat at the berth: master, breakers, neutral, key and glow, START; later shut down in order | Every start-up step in order (the STARTUP duty's checks), engine running then off, master off | Nothing new |
| H2 | **Fuel her up** | RYA L1 "fuel tank"; SBF basic question 49 | Berth by the quay pump, engine off, hose aboard, fill, hose back | Tank fuller, engine off while the hose is aboard | Nothing new (the pump and hose exist) |
| H3 | **Lines and fenders** | RYA L1 "fasten to a cleat", lines and fenders; SBF "cleating with a locking turn" (the cleat only) | Fenders out, roving fender hung, lines off and on in the asked order, fenders in | The order of line and fender changes | Nothing new |
| H4 | **First cast-off** | RYA L1 steer and control speed | Leave, reach the turning basin, come back alongside anywhere, engine off | Steps done, under 4 kn in the pen, no knock over 1 kn | Nothing new |
| H5 | **Stop on course** | SBF "kursgerechtes Aufstoppen"; RYA L2 taking way off | At 4 kn down a marked lane, on the call "stop her": neutral, counter the prop walk, astern until she stands | Stopped within the lane, heading change under 15°, lever back to neutral; stopping distance reported | A shaft boat with prop walk (question 2) |
| H6 | **Turn in the basin** | RYA L2 turning in a confined area, wind on the bow; SBF "Wenden auf engem Raum" | In the walled basin, through 180° with helm over, bursts ahead and astern | Reciprocal within 10°, never out of the basin, no contact, under 3 kn | Nothing new |
| H7 | **Hold off the mark** | RYA L2 holding off; RYA Advanced holding station | Bow within a length of a buoy for 90 s with the wind setting her off | Bow inside the circle with at most two excursions, no contact | Nothing new |
| H8 | **Alongside, wind off** | RYA L2 speed and angle of approach; SBF "Anlegen": rudder and engine only, stopped out of gear | Port side to at the named berth, wind off the quay: fenders out, 30 to 45°, way off, a touch astern, bow line first | Speed at one length off under 1.5 kn, angle at first contact under 45°, knock under 0.5 kn, lever in neutral when the first line goes on, moored parallel at the named berth | Nothing new |
| H9 | **Alongside, wind on** | The harder case; SBF "drifting onto the berth is not allowed" | Same berth, 12 kn blowing on: stop parallel a width off, let her settle on the fenders under control | As H8, plus a "drifted on" fault if she lay in neutral with no helm for the last seconds before touching | Nothing new |
| H10 | **Leaving ahead and astern** | RYA L2 leave ahead or astern; SBF "Ablegen" with traffic, space, wind | Twice: ahead with a quay block close ahead, astern with one close astern; lines in the order the wind wants | Free, no contact, two lengths clear, fenders in before the pen mouth | Nothing new (the obstacles are quay blocks in the map data) |
| H11 | **Anchor and weigh** | RYA L2 anchoring; SKS anchoring manoeuvre | Into the wind in the anchorage, way off, let go, drop back, watch for SET, hold a minute, check against a transit, heave in | Let go inside the circle under 0.5 kn and within 30° of the wind, set and never dragging, housed at the end, no ahead with chain over the bow; three bottoms (sand, mud, weed) as three runs | Nothing new; scope is automatic (question 5) |
| H12 | **Horn and lights** | SBF manoeuvring signals (one, two, three short); RYA L2 sound signals; nav lights at dusk | On "alter to starboard / to port / go astern", sound the signal, then do it; lights on before leaving at dusk | The blast count from the horn's press timing, then the manoeuvre; lights before "free" after sunset | A short-blast counter (small) |
| H13 | **Low-speed slalom** | RYA L2 low-speed control and windage; SBF steering by marks | Thread a line of marks alternately port and starboard under 3 kn, and back | Gates in order on the right side, no contact | Nothing new (marks in the map data) |
| H14 | **Springing off** | RYA L2 "use springs"; the SBF guideline's own example | Fore spring on, slow ahead against it, helm to the quay, stern swings out, spring slipped, away astern | Stern out past 30° on the spring, no contact beyond the bow fender | **A spring line** in the mooring model (question 3) |
| H15 | **Pick up the mooring** | RYA L2 "approach and secure to a buoy" | Downwind on a transit, way off with the buoy at the shoulder, hook the strop, make fast; then slip and leave | Under 1 kn with the buoy within 2 m of the bow, neutral while working at the side | **A buoy a player can make fast to** (question 4) |

### Course 2: Rescue practice (RYA L2 and Advanced emergencies; the SBF rescue manoeuvre)

| # | Exercise | Practises | The player | Passed when | Needs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | **Man overboard, into wind** | RYA L2 into-wind method; SBF: disengage at once, stern away, gear ready, side named, stopped with the prop stopped | At 6 kn outside the breakwater, "man overboard, port": neutral, stern away, round, stop 6 to 8 lengths downwind, head to wind dead slow, casualty at the shoulder on the stated side, engine off, ring, haul in | Neutral within 3 s; stern turned away in the first 5 s; approach within 30° of into-wind; stop with the casualty a third to a half back from the bow; **out of gear whenever the swimmer is within the prop guard (a strike fails the run)**; aboard | A survivor placed by the exercise (the rescue survivor exists; the placing is new, small) |
| R2 | **Man overboard, drift down** | RYA L2 drift-down method (both are needed at L2) | Stop 2 to 4 lengths upwind beam-on and drift down, engine off before contact | Stopped in the band, beam-on within 30°, neutral for the last seconds, same prop rule | As R1 |
| R3 | **Take her in tow** | RYA Advanced towing in confined waters; Safety Boat towing astern | A disabled skiff drifting outside: approach from leeward, pass the line, take the load up gently, 4 kn to the entrance, shorten on the winch, bring her alongside | Attached without a knock, peak line load under a share of breaking load, under 5 kn, shortened before the entrance, the line never parts | A second boat spawned dead by the exercise (the tow and multi-boat code exist) |
| R4 | **Fire on a fishing boat** | RYA L2/Advanced fire fighting | The existing fire job as a timed exercise | Fire out in time, no knock on the casualty, own boat not alight | Nothing new |
| R5 | **Fog in the pen** | RYA L2 restricted visibility, IRPCS 19 and 35; SBF signals in reduced visibility | Fog preset: lights on, leave, a prolonged blast every two minutes, under 3 kn, out by compass to the entrance pair, back by the reciprocal | The fog duty's own checks with no missed window, lights before "free", speed cap, no contact | Nothing new |
| R6 | **Alarm under way** | SBF basic questions 53 to 57 (overheating, lamps); RYA Advanced fault finding | The exercise seeds an overheating engine: recognise the lamp, throttle back, shut down before the derate becomes a shutdown | The lamp seen (throttle back within the window), engine stopped before the cut-out | A seeded fault (the thermal model exists; small) |
| R7 | **Search and recover** | RYA Advanced search patterns | "Somewhere north of the east cardinal, last seen ten minutes ago", visibility 400 m: an expanding square on called courses and legs, sight, recover as R1 | First four legs within 15° and 20 % of what was called; recovered in time; the prop rule | A seeded drifting survivor (small) |

### Course 3: Coast practice (RYA L2 Section C and Intermediate; SBF See compass, bearing and marks)

| # | Exercise | Practises | The player | Passed when | Needs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | **Steer the course** | SBF "Steuern nach Kompass" (steady over a distance, changes at once, a bigger number is a turn to starboard) | Outside at 6 kn: "270", then "300", then "240", each held for 400 m; repeat on the handset | Within 5° for 80 % of each leg after settling; each change begun within 10 s and in the right direction | Nothing new |
| C2 | **Steer for the mark** | SBF steering by marks and landmarks; RYA Advanced transits and buoyed channels | Named marks by shape and colour, then the leading line into the pen with the two whites in line | Bearing to the mark within 10° of the heading; on the transit under 5 m off the line for the last 200 m | Nothing new (the transit is computed from the two lights' positions) |
| C3 | **Where are we?** | SBF "Peilen" (single or cross bearing, a position fixed); RYA L2 Section C hand-bearing compass | Stopped outside: bearings of two charted beacons about 90° apart, the two lines on the paper chart, the fix read off | Each bearing within 3°, the fix within 50 m of the true position | **A bearing readout** (in the binoculars first; a held hand-bearing compass if wanted) and **a small "fix" intent**, because pencil marks live outside the world state (question 6) |
| C4 | **The slalom at speed** | RYA L2 S- and U-turns at planing speed | On the rescue boat or a Shipyard planing boat: eight marks outside alternately, U-turn round the end mark, back, off the plane before the entrance | Gates in order, above planing speed for the run, no mark closer than 3 m, under the cap by the entrance | Nothing new; no trim or seating is scored (none is modelled) |
| C5 | **Read the marks** | RYA L2 buoyage; SBF See lateral and cardinal marks | Into the pen: leave the isolated danger clear, pass the cardinal set on the safe side of the wreck, enter red to port | Track never inside the danger circles, each cardinal passed on its safe side, red to port, no grounding | Nothing new |
| C6 | **Set and drift** | RYA L2 approach in a stream, Intermediate course to steer; SBF See set and drift | Across the pen's current zone between two marks, ferry gliding; then again at slack | Under 10 m off the line for 80 % of the crossing, arrival within 10 m | Nothing new (one current zone in the map data) |
| C7 | **Lights at night** | RYA Advanced night cruising; SBF See light characters | The same entry at 23:00: marks by rhythm (Q, Q(3) 10s, Fl R 4s, Fl G 4s), the leading lights, berth by the quay lamps, nav lights on, panel in red | As C5 plus lights on throughout and the berthing numbers; a three-choice question on the duty board, "what is the light at two o'clock?" | A three-choice question item (small) |

### The Examiner's Run (closes courses 1 and 2)

One continuous run of ten to fifteen minutes in the SBF's form: the examiner's voice names the manoeuvres in a seeded order (leave, stop on course, turn in the basin, a horn signal, man overboard, alongside; the compass course and the bearing once course 3 is open). Compulsory items must pass by the second attempt; two of the three "other" items must pass; a prop strike, a knock over 2 kn, or gear engaged with the swimmer in reach ends it. "Again, please" gives the second attempt. The result card lists each item passed or not, with the attempt and the reason, and the badge by total fault weight. Recorded and reviewed as one.

### Out, and said so on the Training page

Knots (a hand-tracked rope mini-game is large, a new physics object, and poor on the Quest); the lifejacket (nothing is worn); launching and trailers; tide heights, secondary ports and tidal curves (no rise and fall in the game; a whole-world change if ever); Med-style stern-to mooring and berthing between piles (the mooring model holds only alongside); dinghy and windsurfer rescue; composing a Mayday on the VHF (the radio receives; a quiz question at most); the lights of tugs and trawlers by night (other boats carry plain lights); trim tabs and power trim; the written papers (the game's own few questions only; neither catalogue is reproduced).

### The boat

Harbour and Rescue practice on a **shaft-and-rudder boat with prop walk**, fitted with fenders and a life ring (question 2 decides which). The skiff for the tiller variants of H5, H6 and R1 (RYA's "tiller steering"). The rescue boat (150 hp outboard) for C4. The tug, a twin-screw boat and the pilot boat for P5. One `CONFIG.training.boat` default and a per-exercise override; the exercise launches and withdraws boats as the second-boat code already does.

## 4. The training harbour: one data file

**As built in M1** (`src/world/regions/training.ts`): Harbour Head with the harbour cut into its north shore, 6 m of water throughout (a bank under the whole harbour keeps the breakwater's rubble out of it); a breakwater with a 56 m entrance facing north, Fl R 4s and Fl G 4s on its heads and a lateral pair outside; a 260 m fendered quay with Berth A (fuel), B (the start) and C; the slot (two short jetties 25 m apart, about 6 m clear at each end of the fishing boat); the dock (two quay walls 30 m apart, 32 m long, open to the north); the stopping lane between four yellow special marks (280 by 30 m); an office and a shed. The anchorages, the slalom line, the wreck and its cardinals, the leading lights, the beacons, the current and the fog bank come with the milestones that use them.

A region is a data file, never code of its own (the standing rule), so the pen is `src/world/regions/training.ts`, about 1.5 by 1.5 km, and one line in `regions/index.ts`. The exercises above say exactly what it needs:

- **Water:** a sheltered inner pen (a `shelter` circle, so it is calm whatever the weather), a dredged turning basin walled with fendered quay (a `cut` and `blocks`), one open stretch outside the breakwater for MOB, the slalom and the rough-day entry, one `fogBank` outside, one small `currents` zone across the outer channel (1.5 kn at peak, slack by phase).
- **Berths:** a long fendered quay with four named berths, an unfendered T-head (the exposed berth), a short pontoon berth, a slipway, the start berth at a harbour port; three anchorage ports with sand, mud and weed bottoms; a fuel pump on the quay.
- **Marks:** a port and starboard pair at the entrance; a cardinal set round a charted wreck; an isolated-danger mark over a rock with a `shoals` entry; eight special marks in a slalom line outside and a marked stopping lane in the basin; a leading-line pair of fixed white lights on 000°; two charted beacons for bearings. Quay lamps for the night exercises, one shed, a planning table and a review board on the quay.
- **Not in it:** harbour boats, a town, trees, ferries, traffic, jobs and calls (`quietCalls`). Weather mostly Clear; exercises set wind, fog and time of day through the existing environment intents (`weatherManual`, `weatherForce`, `timeOfDay`), host-authoritative, so crew see the same.

**Cost on the Quest:** the sea (104k triangles on every map) and your own boat (55 to 120 draw calls) are the floor everywhere; the pen adds one merged structures mesh, a dozen instanced marks and a few lamps, so it should cost less than the home world (41 to 73 draw calls, about 160k triangles), far less than a coast map (150 to 176 draw calls, 200k to 360k triangles). Nothing streams; it loads at once. Measured in headless Chromium with the debug overlay before each push; a Quest try after.

A later "real harbour" exercise (P2) reuses Oban or Tayvallich as they are; the pen is never a coast package (a package cannot be trimmed to one basin without a pipeline run, and brings the town and the harbour boats).

## 5. How it works inside

**As built in M1:** `training.html` sets `TRAINING` in `src/mode.ts`; `MODE` stays `'voyage'` there, so the page is a Voyage in every respect (no career, no saves, the career-save guard in `careerSession.ts` already holds) and the map id is `training`, which `world/regions/index.ts` answers with the training harbour alone (it is not on the Voyage map list). Crew joining a training host are sent to `training.html`. The rest of this section is the plan as written.

**A page and a flag, not a fork.** `training.html` (a copy of `voyage.html`, title only) picks the mode through `src/mode.ts` as Voyage does, with `?exercise=` as Voyage has `?map=`; Vite's page list and the front-page tile; `regions/index.ts` returns the pen; the start berth from the pen. The world is created with the voyage flag (no money, no wear, no career) plus `world.training` for what differs. **First of all,** `careerSession.ts` must skip restore and save for training as it does for voyage: every address shares one browser storage, and a training page left as career would load and overwrite the owner's career slots. The two-way `MODE === 'voyage'` tests (about 54 in ten files) are each looked at; crew joining a training host are sent to the right page (`samePageAsHost`, `build.mode`).

**The exercise engine** (`src/training/`) generalises the first-voyage tutorial: an `ExerciseDef` has a brief, a start (berth or spot, boat, wind, time of day), **steps** (each a plain check on the world, announced once on the VHF and ticked on the duty board, with an optional time limit) and **rules** (continuous checks, each a fault with a weight of 1, 2 or 3 = fail: the speed cap, a knock, fenders in on the approach, lights off after dark, gear engaged near a swimmer, a missed fog blast, a mark on the wrong side). `TrainingState` is small plain JSON on `WorldState.training`, stepped in `stepWorld` beside the tutorial, driven by intents (`trainingStart`, `trainingRestart`, `trainingAbort`, `trainingRetry`), so crew and an instructor see it in snapshots for free. Nothing in it touches Three.js, the DOM, VR or the desktop; the later touchscreen version inherits it. Scoring reads the state the game has: speed, `VesselState.contact` directly (the wear model waits for a settling grace, which would miss the first seconds of every run), mooring and anchor phases, fenders, lights, the horn's press timing, recovery phases, distance to marks, `world.time` (never the wall clock or the game clock, which sleep and the time scale move). It scores the boat the trainee is aboard (`boatOf`), not the home boat.

**Tunables** in `CONFIG.training` with a comment giving the syllabus reason for each: the pen speed cap, the knock and berthing limits, approach speed and angle, the MOB windows and prop guard, course and bearing tolerances, time limits, fault weights and badge thresholds, the sample rate and keyframe interval of the recording, the boat per course.

**Tests:** the exercise engine drives two exercises through `stepWorld` with intents in Node (as `tests/tutorial.test.ts` does), the page test stubs `training.html` (as `voyageMode.test.ts`), the regions test keeps the pen's ids unique, and a determinism test covers the recording's row and a replay round trip.

## 6. Recording and review

**As built in M2** (`src/training/recording.ts`, `runStore.ts`, `review.ts`, `reviewPanel.ts`): as below, with two differences. The run list and the review's controls are in the Training tab (Runs), and the review has a picture of its own (the harbour's chart with the quays drawn, on the desktop at the side of the screen, in VR a panel in front of you) rather than drawing on the paper chart and the plotter: it keeps the Training area out of the chart code and shows the quays the chart leaves out. The card on a quay board is left to the menu's card, which VR shows on the wrist. A run is a player's own: the page that runs the world records it.

**What is recorded,** by the host's page only (it has the 60 Hz state): three streams per run. **Samples** at 10 Hz (every sixth tick): one fixed row of numbers per boat and per player, the fields the views read, so a replay is a living boat, not a silent pose: position, orientation, linear and angular velocity, lever, helm, rudder, thrust, engine rpm and phase, lights, horn, fenders, mooring and anchor phases, the player's boat, position, yaw and head. About 160 bytes a row: 1.2 MB for twenty minutes, under 0.5 MB gzipped. **A whole-world keyframe** every 30 s (3 to 18 KB, carrying weather, time, lines, cargo, the exercise state). **Events** on `world.time`: attempt start and end, step done, fault with its weight and text, contact with its speed, horn blast, radio line, recovery and anchor phases, the result.

**Where it lives:** never inside `WorldState` (the delta packer would resend a growing array whole at 20 Hz) and never in `localStorage` (about 5 MB, shared with the career saves on every address). A host-side recorder, like the chart marks, appends to typed arrays and flushes to IndexedDB under `swvr.training.*` every few seconds, off the frame; a run list in the Training tab; download and load as a gzipped file; the oldest runs cleaned up past a limit. A layout version in the run header, so later builds still read old runs.

**Why samples and not intents:** replaying a run by re-applying the player's inputs would need the simulation to be the same forever; weather, traffic and the career are seeded from time, and every push changes the physics a little (the golden tests exist to catch drift of a millionth), so old runs would diverge within seconds.

**The review, in two steps.** First (M2) **the chart review**, which costs nothing on the Quest: the track drawn on the paper chart and the plotter, coloured by speed, with pins for the events; a scrub marker along it showing that instant's numbers (speed, heading, lever, rudder, distance off the berth face); attempt one and two drawn together; and **the card** (the Training tab, and a board on the quay readable in VR): passed or try again with the reason, the numbers that matter for that exercise (approach speed at one length, angle at first contact, biggest knock; stopping distance and heading change; turn radius; time to neutral, time to recovery, closest the swimmer came to the propeller; heading error per leg; cross-track; peak tow load), the fault list with times. Second, **only if the chart review leaves you wanting it** (M7): **a rendered replay**: a `ReplaySession` with the same three members the crew render path uses (`latest`, `renderTime`, `bracket`), so the existing crew path plays the samples back with the boat alive and audible, a scrubber (play, pause, speed, seek, next event), the recording camera on desktop (free fly, ride with her, clean screen), from the deck or the quay in VR, and a ghost of a chosen run as a second boat at far level of detail.

## 7. The instructor, in multiplayer

**Day one, for free:** an instructor joins the trainee's game by code, stands aboard, talks by proximity and VHF voice, takes any control the trainee is not holding, and sees the duty board, the radio log and (once M1 is in) the exercise's steps and faults, because all of it is in the world. On desktop the instructor can be the recording camera (crew see the camera body; a VR trainee can hold it). **The trainee hosts:** the host's page is the one that simulates at 60 Hz and records; the instructor watches at 20 Hz interpolated, which is fine for watching.

**M5 adds:** the training intents open to any player (as launching a boat is), so the instructor sets, resets and aborts exercises for the trainee; an `instructor` field in the state that quiets the scripted voice when a human gives the calls (the MOB drop is then the instructor's intent, not a timer); the second-boat menu gates opened for training, so the instructor can launch a boat at a pen berth and stand off (far level of detail when away) or play the casualty in R3. **Not planned:** dual controls or an override (first hand keeps the control), an instructor-only view (everything in the world is seen by all), a separate instructor page.

## 10. Flagged as unwise

- Naming anything after the RYA, the DMYV or the DSV, or printing anything resembling their certificates or the Praxisprotokoll; reproducing the question catalogues or the G20 text.
- Replaying by re-running inputs; keeping the recording in the world state or in `localStorage`.
- Building the mooring buoy, springs, hip towing, knots, trim tabs or the kill cord silently to tick a syllabus box: each is new rope, body or control work under the standing rule; each is a question above.
- A tide-height model for the sake of Coast practice: a whole-world change far beyond training.
- Give-way behaviour in the AI traffic and a judge of the player's crossing conduct: the traffic keeps course and speed by design; a main-game change if ever.
- Using a coast map as the pen; a ghost or an instructor's boat at near level of detail when standing off.
- Judging what the state cannot show (crew seated, lookout kept, kill cord worn, trim set): not scored rather than faked.
