- **Was:** a litre a kW of her power (every boat about 4.5 hours flat out, whatever her size: a 6 m RIB 200 L, a 14 m pilot boat 900 L), but the stern trawler's 18 m³ and the harbour tug's 60 m³ (days).
- **Real boats** (checked 31 Oct 2026): 14 m pilot boats 1,500-2,300 L; 32-36 ft gillnetters 1,300-1,500 L; a 12 m Damen Stan Tug 1205 5.8 m³; 20 m stern trawlers about 20-30 m³; a 6 m RIB 100-150 L. All days at sea: far more than a game wants.
- **Now** (parts/engineRoom.ts, `CONFIG.shipyard.fuel`): hours flat out at her full power, growing with her size, from `minHours` 1 at `smallT` 1.5 t (her lines' displacement as drawn) to `maxHours` 6 at `largeT` 150 t and up, as the logarithm of her tonnage; the tank is those hours of her engines' consumption (diesel or petrol). So a bigger, more powerful boat has a bigger tank, and lasts longer flat out. As they come: a 6 m RIB 30 L (1 h), a 9 m landing craft 170 L (2.5 h), a gillnetter 240 L, a 14 m pilot boat 800 L (4 h), a small tug 560 L, a fast catamaran 680 L, a 20 m stern trawler 1,100 L (6 h), the harbour tug 5,400 L (6 h). - **The type cards and the brief** (typeFit.ts): each fit's range flat out rescaled by her new tank over the one it was measured on. A brief's range test asks less (`brief.test.ts`, the potting brief 50 nm).
- **Two fleet boats it moved:** the fast landing craft (fleet `lander`) went from 450 L to 250 L, which put her centre of gravity 1.4 cm higher, and her righting arm, which had only just reached 60° (8 mm there), ran out at 59°. Her beam is a step wider (`src/boats/fleet/lander.json` beam 8 to 9): it reaches past 60° again, with 17 mm there. ### The foiling ferry on a choppy sea: she flies level over the waves; geared to take off in a Moderate sea (28 Oct 2026; on next for the owner to try)

Owner, 28 Oct 2026: "maybe a nice test to do for the hydrofoils is take them out on a choppy sea, and obviously they should be pretty much completely smooth because they ride over the waves." Then, on what it found: "You do it here" (the flight computer's change) and "Yes, 1.6:1" (her gearing).

- **What the test found:** she wasn't smooth. Her flight computer held her forward foil a set depth under the *local* wave surface, so she rose and fell with every wave ("contouring"): about 1.65 m/s² rms (0.17 g) of up-and-down at her helm into a Slight sea, more than she felt running on her hulls in it. And in a Moderate sea she couldn't get over the hump into the waves or across them (stuck on her hulls at 10 to 12 kn): her motors ran out of torque at low speed with the 1.33 : 1 reduction matched for top speed.
