**Covers:** everything on the test build since the 23 October notes (Friday afternoon), from every chat's work, up to Monday 26 October.

**What's where:** all of this is on the test build at https://tartanski-cpu.github.io/SWVR/next/. ## Highlights

- **Construction projects:** build a wind farm in six stages. **Tarran Rig**, two turbines north of Calder Haven: survey the site, lay its marks, tow out the monopiles, the transition pieces, guard the works while the turbines go up, and bring her on line. When it's done the farm stands for good.
- **Crew transfer to wind farms:** the **Holm Rig** wind farm, technicians to put across onto a turbine's boat landing, and a bow fender to hold her there.
- **The harbour tug:** a 25 m azimuth stern drive tug, a one-off in the Shipyard, with heavy fendering, her own Z-drives and twist-and-throttle levers.
- **Diving:** eight wrecks charted for the submersible game, and a dive support vessel with an A-frame and a submersible: DIVE at a wreck opens the submersible game.
- **Your boats on the quay:** a Shipyard sign at every career harbour and a board of your favourites beside it. Career boats are bought at the yard, after a five-minute sea trial.
- **Weather and sea:** set the weather by hand (wind, rain, waves up to about 6 m), and short, steep seas in enclosed water.
- **Twin engines** worked separately, each with its own lever and START.
- **Fixes:** the game no longer drops to a few frames a second beside the boats board, multiplayer copes with a host whose page is out of date, and Step ashore puts you beside your boat.

## Career

- **Construction projects** (Menu → Office → **Projects**, at the Calder Haven office). One project at a time, alongside the ordinary jobs; each job pays, each stage pays a bonus, and the whole project pays £5,000 at the end. What's next shows on Office → **Jobs** and on the duty board.
  1. **Survey the site:** run three survey lines at 2.5 to 6.5 knots, and lay the met buoy.
  2. **Mark the site:** lay a north, east, south and west cardinal with a deck crane.
  3. **Foundations:** tow each monopile from the laydown to its turbine with a tug; a jack-up drives it.
  4. **Transition pieces:** tow the barge of transition pieces to the jack-up, and run out bolts and grout.
  5. **Turbines:** an installation ship puts them up while you stand guard and turn passing ships away; then the fitters go out by crew transfer.
  6. **Commissioning:** a commissioning crew to each turbine; each lights up and turns. Both turning, the project's done and the farm stays.
- **Crew transfer to the Holm Rig wind farm:** jobs come over the radio; take the technicians from the Calder Haven quay, put your bow fender on the turbine's landing and hold her there while they step across and climb. The bow fender is a yard add-on for the beginner boat.
- **Boats cost money now:** take a five-minute sea trial in any of your favourites, or buy her at a yard for the difference in price. The Shipyard opens from the yard on your own boat for a refit.
- **A Shipyard sign at every harbour**, and beside it a **Your boats** board: your saved favourites, a page at a time, each with Sea trial or Buy her.
- **Cargo:** more kinds of cargo, and crane-only loads too heavy to carry (a deck crane lifts them).
- **The front page** says **Continue your career** when you have one saved, with its slot and balance.
- **Brannock Pier** is the deep-water berth: deeper boats start there.

## Voyage

- **The timber run:** the camp's logs come back laid on the beach in Ardantrive Bay, to tow off one at a time (no barge now), and pay £2,000 each at the Oban timber merchant.
- **Boats arrive made fast:** Go and launched boats come in moored at a berth, or at anchor.
- **No career, but damage and money for the session:** jobs pay into the session's account; boats take damage only from real crashes.
- **Landmarks:** Connel Bridge over the Falls of Lora and McCaig's Tower above Oban. Oban's start berth is the North Pier's south-west face.

## Diving: Wreck ROV

- **Eight wreck sites** on the charts: five on the Oban map at real places and depths (12 to 54 m: off Dunstaffnage, the north end of Kerrera, off Slatrach Bay, under Gylen Castle, north-west of Maiden Island) and three in the career islands (8 to 40 m, the Margaret Ann among them). Dashed blue rings on the chartplotter, wreck marks on the paper chart. Each says "Wreck" and her depth until a dive names her: the submersible game builds the wreck itself.
- **A dive support vessel:** a dive A-frame and a small submersible on any boat big enough (the Shipyard's Diving group). Stopped over a wreck with the sub in the sea, **DIVE** opens the submersible game, Wreck ROV, at that wreck.
- **The round trip:** come back from the dive to her deck with your finds in salvage crates, sold in port; the survey and the wreck's story are paid at once. Each site remembers what you took. (Wreck ROV has release notes of its own.)

## Boats, controls and gear

- **The harbour tug:** 25 x 11.2 m, twin 1,850 kW, a one-off hull with heavy black fendering, drawn with Z-drives. Her wheelhouse and deckhouse are yours to shape; she tows over her bow, or over her stern with a stern roller.
- **Azimuth drives** steered by twist-and-throttle levers, one each side, their heads a drum with a bar on top, as real tug consoles.
- **Twin engines** worked separately: a lever and a START each, and a handle to bring both together, on twin shafts and twin stern drives. Each engine is heard on its own. Diesels can stall.
- **The autopilot** is on the console, with PORT 5° and STBD 5°.
- **A TV on the wall** that shows a browser tab to everyone aboard, and **rows of seats** that turn, for a crew-transfer boat.
- **The console screens tidied:** the log reads speed through the water; no rate of turn, no doubled readings.
- **Held controls stay held in VR** (the breakaway taken out); on desktop, W and S work the throttle while the mouse holds the wheel.
- **Step ashore and aboard by pointing** at an amber ring over the quay or her deck.
- **The deckhouse stair** walked as a smooth slope; the stern trawler's deckhouse runs on aft, up to 2.5 m high.
- **The deck crane** says why HOOK won't let go ("lower it to the water first"), sets a load on the nearest clear patch of deck, and its load moves as smoothly as the crane.

## The Shipyard

- **Her finish:** as new, worked or well used (the hull's weathering: grime at the waterline, rust runs, faded paint).
- **The Paint step** in three groups, with more colours (14 hull paints).
- **Handles on the 3D view** for the whole Hull step; the catamaran as a hull type; high window sills; faceted aft windows; a plumb stem drawn whole.
- **Scratch boats:** start without a name and nothing is saved until you press 💾 Save.

## The sea and the weather

- **Weather by hand** (Debug): wind, direction, rain and waves, held until Live weather.
- **The biggest seas** up to about Hs 6 m in open water.
- **Short, steep seas** in enclosed water, long ones in the open.
- **Smoother:** no hitches from the short seas, and harbour boats, buoys and marks cheaper to float.

## Playing together

- **An out-of-date host updates itself.** On a day of updates, a crew-mate with a newer page now makes the host's page reload and host the same game again, the crew following, instead of being turned away.
- **No frozen figures:** a crew-mate who leaves, reloads or closes the page is taken out of the host's world at once, and one whose page stops answering after a short while.
